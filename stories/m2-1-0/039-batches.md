# ST-039 — Group jobs into batches with batch continuations

**Milestone:** M2 · **Epic:** m2-1-0 · **Priority:** must · **Depends on:** ST-038

## Context

Adds batches, which hosting REQ-36 puts in M2: a named group of jobs stored in one transaction (REQ-177) that becomes Succeeded when every member has succeeded (REQ-179), or Completed when every member has finished and one did not succeed (REQ-180), and that then releases its batch-level continuations, OnBatchSucceeded and OnBatchCompleted (REQ-181). A Completed batch is not final: members that are requeued and succeed upgrade it (SCN-119). The story also closes the last ways a continuation could wait for something that no longer exists, a deleted or removed parent and a removed batch (REQ-188), and removes a finished batch with its members once each is past its retention (REQ-192).

Builds on ST-038 (continuations and the cancellation cascade, REQ-171 to REQ-175), ST-020 (requeue and delete, REQ-141, REQ-143, REQ-144), ST-021 (retention: REQ-145's batch-membership clause was built there against batch records written into the store directly, and now meets real batches) and ST-019 (unique keys on members, REQ-150, REQ-152). REQ-188 is here rather than in ST-038 because it names REQ-192's batch removal. It keeps LIV-102 holding, whose delete and removal paths this story closes, and INV-118, which REQ-192's removal of dead-lettered members must log. ST-033 built the job page's batch relations (dashboard REQ-309) against what existed; ST-069 draws the batch graph (dashboard REQ-317), ST-043 applies concurrency keys to batch creation in Inline mode (INV-137's notes) and ST-054 puts these rules in the conformance suite.

Lives in the Bellhop core package (TECH-112) and Bellhop.PostgreSql (TECH-37): a batch, its state and a member's batchId are new stored state, made on both stores that exist (TECH-31). The entry point is IJobClient's CreateBatchAsync(name, jobs, continuations) (TECH-19), and a member's JobContext exposes its batch id (TECH-16). What CreateBatchAsync returns and how an application reads a batch's state are not in the specs (open question 3). Retention lengths come from REQ-104 and are judged with a fake TimeProvider (TECH-12).

## Acceptance criteria

### AC-1 · REQ-177 · must

> When an application creates a named batch of jobs, the system shall store the batch and all its member jobs in one store transaction.
>
> *Transition:* Batch: (new) → Open · *Actor:* Application
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06: a batch is complete at creation; members cannot be added later (REQ-178 retired).

- Given a name, jobs j1 and j2 and an OnBatchSucceeded continuation c, when the application calls CreateBatchAsync (TECH-19), then the batch is stored as open with j1 and j2 as its members, each member carries the batch's id and its JobContext gives that id when it runs (TECH-16), and c is stored as awaiting when the call returns.
- Given one member is rejected, because its unique key is held under the Error policy (REQ-152, whose notes say rejecting the call creates none of a batch's jobs) or its payload is larger than PayloadHardLimit (REQ-148, which stores nothing), then the call fails and neither the batch nor any member is stored.
- Given the store write fails part way, by a fault injected after part of the batch is written or a PostgreSQL connection dropped before the commit, then neither the batch nor any member is stored.
- Concurrency: workers on two nodes claim continuously while batches are created; no member is claimed before the batch and all its members are stored.
- Inspection: IJobClient offers no way to add a job to an existing batch (TECH-19's notes: there is no AddToBatch).
- Whether c is written in the same transaction, and a batch with no jobs, are open questions 1 and 2.

### AC-2 · REQ-179 · must

> While a batch is open or completed, when every member job has succeeded, the system shall mark the batch succeeded.
>
> *Transition:* Batch: Open | Completed → Succeeded · *Actor:* system
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06: Succeeded when every member succeeded. A Completed batch is upgraded to Succeeded if its failed members are later requeued and succeed; its OnBatchSucceeded continuations fire then (REQ-181). Each continuation still fires at most once.

- Given an open batch with members j1 and j2, when the last of them succeeds, then the batch is marked succeeded (SCN-114).
- Given a completed batch, when its members that did not succeed are requeued (REQ-141) and all succeed, then the batch is upgraded to succeeded (SCN-119).
- Given any member is still scheduled, enqueued, processing, failed, awaiting or unroutable, or has been dead-lettered, cancelled or deleted, then the batch is not marked succeeded; a deleted member counts as not succeeded (jobs A-12).
- Given the batch is already succeeded, when a member is requeued (REQ-143) and succeeds again, then nothing is marked or released a second time (the notes: each continuation fires at most once).
- Property: over random member outcomes, requeues and deletes, a batch moves to succeeded only at a step where every member created with it is succeeded.

### AC-3 · REQ-180 · must

> While a batch is open, when every member job is succeeded, dead-lettered, cancelled or deleted and at least one did not succeed, the system shall mark the batch completed.
>
> *Transition:* Batch: Open → Completed · *Actor:* system
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06: Completed means every member finished and at least one did not succeed. It is not final: see REQ-179. Resolved after refiner-model 2026-11-06 F-10 (recommended option): a member deleted explicitly (REQ-144) counts as finished and not succeeded, so the batch still completes.

- Given an open batch, when its last unfinished member is dead-lettered, cancelled or deleted (REQ-144) and at least one member did not succeed, then the batch is marked completed.
- Given every member succeeded, then the batch is marked succeeded (REQ-179), never completed.
- Given a member has failed and will retry (REQ-129), or is still enqueued, processing, awaiting or unroutable, then the batch stays open.
- Given one member is deleted while the others succeed, then the batch completes rather than staying open (the owner's note).
- Given a completed batch, when a requeued member is dead-lettered again, then the batch stays completed and no OnBatchCompleted continuation is enqueued again; this rule applies only while the batch is open.

### AC-4 · REQ-181 · must

> While a batch-level continuation is awaiting, when its batch succeeds, or, for OnBatchCompleted only, its batch completes, the system shall enqueue the continuation.
>
> *Transition:* Job: Awaiting → Enqueued · *Actor:* system
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06: an OnBatchSucceeded continuation stays Awaiting while its batch is Completed, because the batch can still become Succeeded (REQ-179). It can therefore wait for ever, which LIV-102 will report; an operator can cancel it (REQ-134).

- Given an awaiting OnBatchSucceeded continuation, when its batch succeeds, from open or by an upgrade from completed, then the continuation is enqueued exactly once (SCN-114, SCN-119).
- Given an awaiting OnBatchCompleted continuation, when its batch completes, then it is enqueued; when its batch succeeds without having completed, then it is enqueued too.
- Given an awaiting OnBatchSucceeded continuation, when its batch completes, then it stays awaiting (the owner's note), and an operator can cancel it (REQ-134).
- Given an OnBatchCompleted continuation that was enqueued when its batch completed, when the batch is later upgraded to succeeded, then it is not enqueued again.
- Given a batch-level continuation that an operator has cancelled, when its batch succeeds, then it stays cancelled.

### AC-5 · REQ-188 · must

> While a continuation is awaiting, when its parent is deleted or removed by retention (REQ-145, REQ-192), or, for a batch-level continuation, its batch is removed (REQ-192), the system shall cancel the continuation.
>
> *Transition:* Job: Awaiting → Cancelled · *Actor:* system
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06, so a deleted parent never leaves a continuation hanging (LIV-102). Resolved after refiner-model 2026-11-06 F-9: removal by retention is included, because a dead-lettered parent now keeps its OnSuccess continuations awaiting until it is requeued or removed. Run 2 F-5: an OnBatchSucceeded continuation of a batch that stays Completed is cancelled when the batch is removed (REQ-192). Run 3 F-3 (recommended option): a member removed together with its batch (REQ-192) counts as removed by retention, so its own awaiting continuations are cancelled rather than left waiting for a parent that no longer exists.

- Given an awaiting continuation c of parent p, when p is deleted (REQ-144), then c is cancelled, and every awaiting continuation below c with it (REQ-173; jobs A-10).
- Given an OnSuccess continuation of a dead-lettered parent, when the fake clock passes RetentionDeadLettered (REQ-104) and retention removes the parent (REQ-145), then the continuation is cancelled.
- Given a member of a batch that REQ-192 removes, then the member's own awaiting continuations are cancelled (the owner's note: such a member counts as removed by retention).
- Given an awaiting batch-level continuation of a batch that stays completed, when the batch is removed (REQ-192), then the continuation is cancelled.
- Given c is not awaiting, because it was enqueued or cancelled, when p is deleted, then c is unchanged.
- Property: over random sequences that include deletes, retention passes and batch removals, no continuation is ever left awaiting a parent or a batch that is no longer stored (the model's Inv_NoAwaitingOnRemoved); with INV-138 (ST-038), no new one can be added to them.
- The reason recorded for this cancellation is open question 5.

### AC-6 · REQ-192 · must

> While a batch is completed or succeeded, when every member job that is still stored has been succeeded, cancelled or dead-lettered for longer than that state's retention, the system shall remove the batch together with those member jobs, their state history, captured logs and parameters.
>
> *Transition:* Batch: Completed | Succeeded → (removed) · *Actor:* system
>
> *Rationale:* REQ-145 keeps the members of a completed batch so it can still be upgraded (REQ-179), and nothing ended that protection: a batch with one dead letter nobody requeued was kept for ever, payloads and logs included.
>
> *Notes:* refiner-model 2026-11-06 run 2 F-5 (recommended option). Members stay requeueable until every one of them is past its own retention, measured from when it last finished, so requeuing a member postpones the removal. Covers succeeded batches too, whose members REQ-145 removes one by one: the batch record goes with the last of them. Awaiting batch-level continuations are cancelled by REQ-188.

- Given a completed batch whose stored members are all succeeded, cancelled or dead-lettered, when the fake clock passes each member's retention (RetentionSucceeded, RetentionCancelled or RetentionDeadLettered, 7, 7 and 30 days by default, REQ-104), each measured from when that member last finished, then the batch and those members are removed with their state history, captured logs and parameters.
- Given one stored member is not yet past its retention, then nothing of the batch is removed. Given a member is requeued and finishes again, then the removal waits until it is past its retention from that new finish (the notes).
- Given a member is unfinished again after a requeue (enqueued, processing or failed), then nothing of the batch is removed.
- Given an open batch, then this rule removes nothing.
- Given a member was deleted, then it is skipped and does not hold the batch back (jobs A-12).
- Given a succeeded batch, then REQ-145 removes its members as each passes its retention, and once the last of them has passed its retention neither the batch nor any member is stored (the notes: the batch record goes with the last of them).
- Given a batch is removed, then its awaiting batch-level continuations are cancelled (REQ-188), and every dead-lettered member removed with it is logged with its id and name (INV-118).
- Whether a batch is removed in one step is open question 6.

### AC-7 · SCN-114 · scenario

> **Batch success releases its continuation**
>
> *Given:* Batch b has members j1 and j2 and an OnBatchSucceeded continuation c.
>
> 1. REQ-177
> 2. REQ-123 — j1 claimed
> 3. REQ-127 — j1 succeeds
> 4. REQ-123 — j2 claimed
> 5. REQ-127 — j2 succeeds
> 6. REQ-179 — b succeeded
> 7. REQ-181 — c enqueued

> 2. REQ-123 — j1 claimed
> 3. REQ-127 — j1 succeeds
> 4. REQ-123 — j2 claimed

## Tests

Every test carries one `Trait("Req", "<id>")` for each item it verifies.

- Unit, core engine over the in-memory store (TECH-43): REQ-177's shape and its rejections, REQ-179 and REQ-180 for each member outcome, REQ-181 for each condition, REQ-188's delete branch.
- Property, in-memory store with a fake TimeProvider: random member outcomes, requeues, deletes, cancels, retention passes and batch removals, asserting REQ-179's step condition, at most one release per continuation (REQ-179's notes, SCN-114, SCN-119) and REQ-188's "nothing awaits a removed parent or batch".
- Retention, with the fake clock and the cleanup duty: REQ-192 for completed and succeeded batches, a requeue that postpones the removal and a deleted member that is skipped, and REQ-188's retention branches.
- Concurrency, on PostgreSQL with two worker nodes: REQ-177's creation with concurrent claimers and an injected failure before the commit; the last two members finishing at once on different nodes, asserting that the batch changes state once and each continuation is enqueued once.
- Scenario, on both stores: SCN-114 and SCN-119.
- Must keep holding, green on both stores: REQ-145 (members of open and completed batches are kept, now tested with real batches), REQ-150 (a member whose unique key is held), REQ-152 (a member rejected under the Error policy stores nothing of the batch), TECH-19 (CreateBatchAsync on IJobClient, no static entry point), LIV-102 (ST-038's property, which gains the delete and removal actions) and INV-118 (every dead-lettered member removed with a batch is logged with its id and name). ST-038's other continuation tests stay green too.
- Not automated: nothing. The retention lengths are judged with the fake clock, not wall time.
