# ST-021 — Clean up finished jobs after their retention

**Milestone:** M1 · **Epic:** m1-core-preview · **Priority:** must · **Depends on:** ST-020

## Context

Keeps the store bounded. Once a job has been succeeded, cancelled or dead-lettered for longer than that state's retention (REQ-104), it is removed with its state history, captured logs and parameters (REQ-145), in batches of at most CleanupBatchSize with a pause between them (REQ-109). A dead letter leaves only through retention or an explicit delete, and every removal leaves a trace (INV-118), and a job's history, logs and parameters live exactly as long as the job (INV-119). Optionally, one worker node at a time cleans up, under a short lease held in the store (REQ-164, a may).

Builds on ST-020 (the explicit delete, REQ-144, the only other way a dead letter leaves), and through it on ST-011 (dead letters), ST-018 (cancelled jobs) and ST-008 (succeeded jobs, and the timestamped state history of INV-116 that a job's time in a state is read from). Retention cleanup is a background duty, so it never runs on a node without the worker (REQ-40, which must keep holding). It must also keep ST-009's INV-114 holding: cleanup keeps running while maintenance is on (INV-114's notes) and never claims a job. ST-027 builds on it, and ST-030 reruns its store-level tests on PostgreSQL. ST-038 and ST-039 add continuations and batches, and constrain REQ-145; ST-039 also removes a batch together with its members (REQ-192).

Lives in the Bellhop core package (TECH-112) as one of the worker's background duties (TECH-11), over IJobStore (TECH-31) and the in-memory store (TECH-43), with time from `TimeProvider` (TECH-12). The cleanup lease of REQ-164 is a record in the store, not an external lock (TECH-51, hosting REQ-37). The retention options carry the glossary names RetentionSucceeded, RetentionCancelled and RetentionDeadLettered. Batches arrive with ST-039 and captured logs with ST-045, so REQ-145's batch-membership clause and the removal of captured logs are built against what exists: tests write a batch record with its member jobs, and captured log lines, into the store directly. The metric that INV-118's notes call for needs the Bellhop meter, which ST-028 adds later (open question 3).

## Acceptance criteria

### AC-1 · REQ-104 · must

> The system shall retain jobs per state for RetentionSucceeded = 7 days, RetentionCancelled = 7 days and RetentionDeadLettered = 30 days by default.
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06 (§9 Q5): Succeeded jobs are kept 7 days instead of 24 h, for more debugging history at the cost of a larger table.

- Given no retention is configured, then a job that succeeded or was cancelled is still stored while less than 7 days have passed since it entered that state, and is removed by a cleanup run after 7 days have passed (REQ-145); for a dead-lettered job the same holds with 30 days.
- Given RetentionSucceeded is configured as 1 day, then a succeeded job is removed by a cleanup run after 1 day, while cancelled and dead-lettered jobs keep their defaults. The same holds for RetentionCancelled and RetentionDeadLettered, each on its own, and a value set in code wins over configuration (hosting REQ-17).

### AC-2 · REQ-109 · must

> The system shall remove expired jobs in batches of at most CleanupBatchSize = 500 rows with a short pause between batches.

- Given more than twice CleanupBatchSize expired jobs, when cleanup runs, then no batch removes more than 500 jobs, all of them are removed over successive batches, and each batch after the first starts only after a pause has passed on the fake clock since the previous one ended. How long the pause is, and whether history, log and parameter rows count towards the 500, is open question 2; at most 500 jobs per batch holds under both readings.
- Given fewer than 500 expired jobs, then one batch removes them all.
- The sentence gives CleanupBatchSize as a fixed 500 with no override, so no test configures it.

### AC-3 · REQ-145 · must

> When a job has been succeeded, cancelled or dead-lettered for longer than that state's retention, if the job is not a member of a batch that is open or completed, then the system shall remove the job together with its state history, captured logs and parameters, in batches of at most CleanupBatchSize.
>
> *Transition:* Job: Succeeded | Cancelled | DeadLettered → (removed) · *Actor:* system
>
> *Notes:* Resolved after refiner-model 2026-11-06 F-10 (recommended option): retention never removes a member of a batch that is not succeeded, so the batch can still complete (REQ-180) or be upgraded to succeeded (REQ-179, SCN-119). Run 2 F-5: the exemption ends when the batch itself is removed together with its members (REQ-192).

- Given a job that has been succeeded, cancelled or dead-lettered for longer than that state's retention, when cleanup runs on a worker node, then the job, its state history, its captured logs and its parameters are removed.
- Given a job that has been in its finished state for less than its retention, then cleanup leaves it. Given a job that is Scheduled, Enqueued, Processing, Failed, Awaiting or Unroutable, however old, then cleanup never removes it.
- Given a job that was requeued (REQ-141, REQ-143) and finished again, then its retention counts from the time it last entered its finished state.
- Given an expired job that is a member of a batch that is Open or Completed (written into the store directly), then cleanup leaves it. A member of a batch that is Succeeded is removed like any other job.
- Given a node without the worker, then it never runs cleanup (REQ-40).
- Concurrency: cleanup racing a requeue of the same expired job ends either with the job removed, or with the job Enqueued and still stored. A job that a requeue has made Enqueued is never removed.

### AC-4 · INV-118 · must

> The system shall never remove a dead-lettered job except when its retention expires or someone deletes it explicitly, and log every removal with the job id and name.
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06: RET-3's 'never silently deleted' means 'never without a trace', so the 30-day retention (STO-7) stays and every removal is logged and counted in a metric.

- Property: over random sequences of dead-lettering, single and bulk requeues, deletes of this and other jobs, cleanup runs and clock advances, a dead-lettered job leaves the store only when it has been dead-lettered for longer than RetentionDeadLettered and cleanup ran, or when `DeleteAsync` was called for it (REQ-144). Every such removal is logged with the job's id and name.
- Given a cleanup batch that removes many dead letters, then the id and name of every one of them appear in the log.
- Given a removal, then it is counted in a metric (this item's notes). Which instrument, and whether removals of succeeded and cancelled jobs are logged too, is open question 3.

### AC-5 · INV-119 · must

> The system shall keep a job's state history, captured logs and parameters for exactly as long as the job itself.

- Property: over random sequences of state changes, parameter writes, captured log lines (written directly), requeues, deletes and cleanup runs, observed after every step and between cleanup batches, every stored job has all of its state history, captured logs and parameters, and no history entry, log line or parameter exists for a job that is not stored.
- Concurrency: a reader running beside cleanup and deletes never sees a stored job without its history, or history, logs or parameters left behind by a removed job.

### AC-6 · REQ-164 · may

> When retention cleanup runs, the system shall let at most one worker node clean up at a time by holding a short cleanup lease in the store.

- Concurrency: given three worker nodes over one store whose cleanup runs come due together, then at most one node removes jobs at any moment: a store wrapper records each node's batches, and no two nodes' batches overlap in time. Every expired job is still removed exactly once (REQ-145, INV-118).
- Given the node holding the cleanup lease crashes during a run, then once the lease has expired, another worker node can clean up. How long the lease is, and what a node that finds it held does, is open question 4.
- Given the lease, then it is a record in the store, with no external lock service (TECH-51).
- This item is a may. If delivery does not build it, Notes says so, and REQ-145, INV-118 and INV-119 must still hold with several worker nodes cleaning up at once.

## Open questions

1. **Decide in delivery** · REQ-145: how often does cleanup run, so how long may a job stay stored after its retention has passed? No item says. The criteria assert only that a cleanup run after the retention removes it.
2. **Decide in delivery** · REQ-109: how long is the "short pause" between batches, and does CleanupBatchSize count job rows only, or also each job's history, log and parameter rows? At most 500 jobs per batch holds under both readings.
3. **Decide in delivery** · INV-118: does "log every removal" cover dead letters only, or every job that cleanup removes? And which instrument counts removals (this item's notes), under what name? The Bellhop meter is TECH-98 (ST-028), built after this story, and NFR-55's list does not include a removal counter.
4. **Decide in delivery** · REQ-164: how long is the cleanup lease, and does a node that finds it held skip its run or wait? REQ-164 says only "short".
5. **Decide in delivery** · REQ-104, REQ-145: is a job's time in its state judged against the database clock? INV-105 lists claims, lease expiry and due times only. Over the in-memory store both clocks are the `TimeProvider`; on PostgreSQL (ST-030) they can differ by the skew that hosting REQ-26 warns about.
6. **Decide in delivery** · REQ-145: are the job's secondary-index entries (TECH-32) removed with it? The same gap as ST-020's open question 5 for REQ-144.
