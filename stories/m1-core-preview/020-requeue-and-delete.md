# ST-020 — Requeue and delete jobs

**Milestone:** M1 · **Epic:** m1-core-preview · **Priority:** must · **Depends on:** ST-018, ST-019

## Context

Lets an operator or application put a finished job back to work and remove a job for good. A dead letter is requeued one at a time (REQ-141) or in bulk by name, queue, tag or time range, one atomic step per job, with counts of what was requeued and what was skipped (REQ-142); a succeeded or cancelled job is requeued keeping its history (REQ-143). Each requeue is a fresh start, at attempt zero with the cancellation request cleared. A requeue of anything unfinished, or of a job whose unique key another active job holds, is refused (INV-129), so requeue cannot break INV-100. A delete removes the job with its history, captured logs and parameters and logs the removal (REQ-144), and refuses a processing job, which must be cancelled first (INV-130).

Builds on ST-018 (the cancellation request a requeue clears, and the cancellation INV-130 sends the caller to) and ST-019 (unique keys; INV-100 must keep holding), and through them on ST-011 (dead letters) and ST-008 (claims, which SCN-112 ends with, and the state history of INV-116). It must keep ST-007's TECH-19 holding: `RequeueAsync`, `RequeueDeadLetteredAsync(filter)` and `DeleteAsync` get their behaviour here. ST-021 builds on it (retention, and INV-118's explicit delete). ST-034 adds the dashboard's dead-letters page with bulk requeue (dashboard REQ-311), ST-038 and ST-039 add continuations and their cancellation when a parent is deleted (REQ-188), ST-044 writes tags, and ST-030 reruns the store-level tests on PostgreSQL.

Lives in the Bellhop core package (TECH-112): `RequeueAsync`, `RequeueDeadLetteredAsync(filter)` and `DeleteAsync` on IJobClient (TECH-19, whose notes put bulk requeue there), over IJobStore (TECH-31) and, for the tag filter, IJobIndex (TECH-32). The filter's shape, the result's shape and the errors are not named in the specs (open questions 1 to 3). Tags are written from ST-044 on and captured logs from ST-045 on, so REQ-142's tag filter and REQ-144's removal of captured logs are built against what exists: tests add index entries with IJobIndex and write log lines into the store directly. REQ-143's notes keep continuations cancelled with a requeued parent cancelled; continuations arrive with ST-038, so that clause is built against what exists here (a requeue touches only the job itself) and closed by ST-038, which keeps REQ-143 holding. Placement: REQ-144 is inferred M2, but hosting REQ-36 puts IJobClient, whose DeleteAsync it is, in M1, INV-130 (tagged M1) refuses deleting a processing job, INV-118 (M1) allows explicit deletes, and dashboard INV-301 (M1) lists delete among the operate actions.

## Acceptance criteria

### AC-1 · REQ-141 · must

> When an operator requeues a dead-lettered job through the API or the dashboard, the system shall enqueue the job again with its attempt number reset to zero and its cancellation request cleared.
>
> *Transition:* Job: DeadLettered → Enqueued · *Actor:* Operator
>
> *Notes:* refiner-model 2026-11-06 run 2 F-4 (recommended option): a requeue is a fresh start, so a cancellation requested during an earlier run does not turn the next run's first failure into a cancel (REQ-190, REQ-136).

- Given a dead-lettered job j at its last attempt, with a cancellation request recorded during that run, when an operator calls `RequeueAsync(j)`, then j, under the same id, is Enqueued with its attempt number at zero and no cancellation request, and its state history records the transition (INV-116) after the earlier entries.
- Given that requeued job, when it is claimed, then it runs with attempt 1 (SCN-112); when that run's handler throws a retryable exception, then j is marked failed (REQ-129), not cancelled (REQ-190).
- Given a job that is not succeeded, dead-lettered or cancelled, or whose unique key is held by another active job, then INV-129 refuses the requeue.

### AC-2 · REQ-142 · must

> When an operator requeues dead-lettered jobs in bulk by name, queue, tag or time range, the system shall enqueue each matching dead-lettered job again in its own atomic step with its attempt number reset to zero and its cancellation request cleared, skip any job whose unique key is held by another active job, and return how many were requeued, how many were skipped because their state changed meanwhile and how many were skipped because their unique key is held.
>
> *Transition:* Job: DeadLettered → Enqueued · *Actor:* Operator
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06: per job, not all-or-nothing, so a large set never holds one long transaction. Resolved after refiner-model 2026-11-06 F-4 (recommended option): the unique-key guard of INV-129 applies per job in bulk requeue, with its own count. Run 2 F-4: the cancellation request is cleared, as in REQ-141.

- Given dead-lettered jobs of several names, queues, tags (index entries added with IJobIndex) and times, when an operator requeues dead letters in bulk by one of name, queue, tag or time range, then every matching dead letter is Enqueued with attempt zero and no cancellation request, every other dead letter stays dead-lettered, and the result counts them as requeued.
- Given a matching dead letter whose unique key another active job holds, then it stays dead-lettered and is counted as skipped for its unique key.
- Given two matching dead letters with the same unique key and no active holder, then exactly one is requeued, and the other is skipped for its unique key, because the first now holds it (INV-100).
- Given a matching dead letter that another caller requeues while the bulk requeue runs, then the bulk requeue does not requeue it again, and counts it as skipped because its state changed.
- Given a matching dead letter that another caller deletes while the bulk requeue runs, then it is neither requeued nor recreated, and it is not counted as requeued. Which count it falls under is open question 2.
- Given a store failure part-way through, then the jobs already requeued stay requeued and every other matching job is still dead-lettered: the steps are per job, not all-or-nothing. What the call returns then is open question 2.
- Concurrency: two bulk requeues with overlapping filters at once requeue each matching job once; across both results each job is counted as requeued once and otherwise as skipped. No observer ever sees a job Enqueued with its old attempt number or its cancellation request still recorded.
- How the filters combine, and what the time range applies to, is open question 1.

### AC-3 · REQ-143 · must

> When an application or operator requeues a succeeded or cancelled job, the system shall enqueue the same job again with its attempt number reset to zero and its cancellation request cleared, keeping its earlier history.
>
> *Transition:* Job: Succeeded | Cancelled → Enqueued · *Actor:* Operator
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06: RequeueAsync(id) accepts any finished state (DeadLettered is REQ-141) and refuses the rest (INV-129). refiner-model 2026-11-06 run 2 F-4: the cancellation request is cleared, as in REQ-141. Run 2 F-6 (recommended option, accepted): requeuing a cancelled job runs that job only. Continuations cancelled with it (REQ-173, REQ-174, REQ-175) stay cancelled and must be re-created; a cancel is deliberate and ends the chain below it.

- Given a succeeded job j with state history h, when an application or an operator calls `RequeueAsync(j)`, then j, under the same id, is Enqueued with attempt zero and no cancellation request, and its history still holds h, followed by the requeue (INV-116).
- Given a cancelled job j whose cancellation was requested while it was processing, when it is requeued, then the same holds; the request is cleared, so a retryable failure on its next run marks it failed (REQ-129), not cancelled (REQ-190).
- Given the requeued job is claimed, then it runs with attempt 1.
- Given a job that is not succeeded, dead-lettered or cancelled, or whose unique key is held by another active job, then INV-129 refuses the requeue.

### AC-4 · REQ-144 · must

> When an application or operator deletes a job, the system shall remove the job with its history, captured logs and parameters, and log the removal.
>
> *Transition:* Job: Scheduled | Enqueued | Failed | Succeeded | DeadLettered | Cancelled | Awaiting | Unroutable → (removed) · *Actor:* Operator
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06: any state except Processing, which must be cancelled first (INV-130). Continuations awaiting the deleted job are cancelled (REQ-188). An explicit, logged delete of a dead letter is not the silent deletion RET-3 forbids.

- Given a job that is Scheduled, Enqueued, Failed, Succeeded, DeadLettered, Cancelled or Unroutable, or Awaiting (written into the store directly), with state history, parameters and captured log lines, when an application or an operator calls `DeleteAsync` for it, then the job, its history, its captured logs and its parameters are gone from the store, and a log line records the removal; for a dead letter that line names the job's id and name (INV-118).
- Given a deleted scheduled or failed job, then it never runs: nothing is left to promote when its scheduledAt or retryAt passes (REQ-122, REQ-130).
- Given a deleted job that held a unique key, then the key is free, and a new job with it is created (REQ-107).
- Given a processing job, then INV-130 refuses the delete.
- Concurrency: a delete racing the claim of the same enqueued job ends either with the job removed and never claimed, or with the job Processing and the delete refused (INV-130). No handler ever runs for a job that was removed before its claim.

### AC-5 · INV-129 · must

> If an application or operator requeues a job that is not succeeded, dead-lettered or cancelled, or whose unique key is held by another active job, then the system shall refuse the request with an error and leave the job unchanged.
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06 for the state rule. The unique-key rule follows from INV-100: requeuing would otherwise create a second active job with the same key.

- Given a job that is Scheduled, Enqueued, Processing, Failed or Unroutable, or Awaiting (written into the store directly), when an application or operator calls `RequeueAsync` for it, then the call fails with an error and every attribute of the job is unchanged.
- Given a succeeded, dead-lettered or cancelled job j with key k while another active job holds k, when j is requeued, then the call fails with an error and j is unchanged, still finished.
- Concurrency: two finished jobs with the same key requeued at once, or a requeue racing a new enqueue with that key, leave at most one active job with the key; every losing requeue fails and leaves its job unchanged.
- Property: over random sequences of single and bulk requeues, creating calls with and without keys, and lifecycle steps, a requeue changes a job only when it is succeeded, dead-lettered or cancelled and no other active job holds its key, and INV-100 holds after every step.
- The bulk requeue skips and counts such jobs instead of failing (REQ-142).

### AC-6 · INV-130 · must

> If an application or operator deletes a processing job, then the system shall refuse the request with an error that says to cancel the job first, and leave the job unchanged.
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06.

- Given a processing job j, when an application or operator calls `DeleteAsync(j)`, then the call fails with an error whose message says to cancel the job first, and j is unchanged: still Processing with its lease, attempt and history, and its handler's outcome is written as usual when it ends.
- Given j's cancellation was requested but j is still Processing, then the delete is still refused. Once j has left Processing, for example as Cancelled (REQ-136, REQ-190), the delete removes it (REQ-144).
- Property: over random sequences of deletes, claims, handler outcomes, cancellations and lease recoveries, no delete ever removes a job that is Processing.

### AC-7 · SCN-112 · scenario

> **Requeued dead letter starts again from attempt one**
>
> *Given:* Job j is DeadLettered after 5 attempts.
>
> 1. REQ-141 — operator requeues j
> 2. REQ-123 — claimed
>
> *Then:* j is Processing with attempt 1.

## Open questions

1. **Decide in delivery** · REQ-142: what is the shape of `RequeueDeadLetteredAsync`'s filter? Do name, queue, tag and time range combine, so that all must match, or is it one at a time; which time does the range apply to (when the job was dead-lettered, enqueued or created); and does a name match its aliases (REQ-101)? TECH-19 names only the method.
2. **Decide in delivery** · REQ-142: which type carries the three counts and what are they called; is a match deleted meanwhile counted as "skipped because its state changed" (a deleted job has no state) or not counted at all; and what does the call do when the store fails part-way: throw, losing the counts, or return the counts so far? Because the steps are per job, part of the set may already be requeued.
3. **Decide in delivery** · INV-129, INV-130: which exceptions do a refused requeue and a refused delete throw, and what do their messages say? INV-130 says only that its message says to cancel the job first.
4. **Decide in delivery** · REQ-141, REQ-143, REQ-144: what do `RequeueAsync` and `DeleteAsync` do for a job id that is not stored, for example one already removed by retention? No item says.
5. **Decide in delivery** · REQ-144: are the job's secondary-index entries (IJobIndex, TECH-32: tags and correlation id) removed with it? REQ-144 lists history, captured logs and parameters only, and a stale entry would make a tag search (dashboard REQ-308) return a job that no longer exists.
6. **Decide in delivery** · REQ-141, REQ-142, REQ-143: which reason does the state history record for a requeue? INV-116 needs one for every transition, and no item names one. It is shown on the job's timeline (dashboard REQ-309).
7. **Decide in delivery** · REQ-144: what level, category and wording does the removal log line have? REQ-144 says only "log the removal", and INV-118 adds the job id and name for dead letters only.
