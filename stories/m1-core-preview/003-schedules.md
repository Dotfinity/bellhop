# ST-003 — Evaluate cron and interval schedules in UTC or a local time zone

**Milestone:** M1 · **Epic:** m1-core-preview · **Priority:** must · **Depends on:** ST-002

## Context

Delivers schedule evaluation, the function the recurring stories call to find a job's next occurrence: 5- and 6-field cron expressions, fixed intervals with Every(TimeSpan) and readable helpers such as Cron.Daily(hour: 3) (TECH-23), ISchedule.GetNextOccurrence(after, timeZone) with cron and interval built in and room for an application's own calendars (TECH-25), and time zones identified by IANA id (TECH-24). A schedule is evaluated in UTC unless its job opts into Local time with an explicit zone (REQ-200); daylight-saving rules apply to Local cron schedules (REQ-228, REQ-229) and never to intervals (INV-208). Doing this once, before any recurring story, keeps every node computing the same instants.

Builds on ST-002 (the job contracts, and AddRecurring's schedule parameter, which this story completes; see TECH-22). ST-022 to ST-026 build on it: stored definitions and their nextRunAt (recurring REQ-203, ST-022), the manager's next N occurrences (TECH-21, ST-023) and the trigger and misfire rules (ST-026); ST-047 adds validity windows, ST-053's analyzer for invalid cron literals (TECH-111) checks the same syntax, and ST-072's recurring builder shows it. TECH-23 and TECH-25 are owned before any story builds the recurring module, and TECH-23 before ST-022 and ST-023 use it: they are the contract those stories code against, and REQ-36 puts recurring jobs in M1.

Lives in the core package, Bellhop: ISchedule with GetNextOccurrence(after, timeZone) and its cron and interval implementations (TECH-25), Every(TimeSpan) and the Cron helpers (TECH-23), and the time modes Utc and Local (the recurring glossary's timeMode). The core takes no third-party dependency (TECH-8), so the cron parser and the zone handling are built on the BCL. The cron dialect beyond the field count, the helper set, the parameter and result types and how a time mode and a zone are given are open question 3.

## Acceptance criteria

### AC-1 · REQ-200 · must

> The system shall evaluate a recurring job's schedule in UTC unless its time mode is Local, in which case it is evaluated in the IANA time zone the job names; Utc is the default time mode.
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06: all schedules are UTC (absolute) unless a job opts into Local time, for jobs that must run at a wall-clock hour in one place. Local mode needs an explicit IANA zone and never uses a node's own zone, because nodes in different regions would disagree (REQ-209, REQ-210). Daylight-saving rules apply only to Local jobs (REQ-228, REQ-229).

- Given a schedule whose job sets no time mode, then it is evaluated in UTC: Cron.Daily(hour: 3) gives 03:00 UTC every day, and the result is the same whatever local zone the node's TimeProvider reports (TECH-12).
- Given time mode Local with the zone Europe/Berlin, then Cron.Daily(hour: 3) gives 03:00 Berlin time every day: 02:00 UTC in winter and 01:00 UTC in summer.
- Given Local mode, then the node's own zone is never used: evaluations on nodes whose TimeProvider reports different local zones give the same instants (REQ-200's notes).
- Local mode without a valid IANA zone is rejected by recurring REQ-209 (ST-023) and REQ-210 (ST-022).

### AC-2 · INV-208 · must

> Where a recurring job has an interval schedule, the system shall fire it at fixed elapsed-time intervals, whatever its time mode and across daylight-saving changes.
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06: Local mode only affects cron-style 'at a wall-clock time' schedules.

- Property: for random intervals, start instants, IANA zones that observe daylight saving and both time modes, the occurrences found by calling GetNextOccurrence from each previous occurrence are exactly one interval of elapsed time apart, across spring and autumn changes.
- Given Every(TimeSpan.FromHours(1)) in Local mode for Europe/Berlin across the autumn change, then two occurrences fall in the repeated local hour, one hour of elapsed time apart, unlike an hourly cron schedule (REQ-229).
- Given the same interval, then GetNextOccurrence returns the same instant whichever time zone it is given.
- What an interval is anchored to when after is not an occurrence is open question 2.

### AC-3 · REQ-228 · must

> Where a recurring job's time mode is Local, when its next occurrence falls on a local time skipped by a daylight-saving change, the system shall run it once at the next valid instant.

- Given Local mode for Europe/Berlin and a daily cron at 02:30, when the next occurrence falls on the spring change (02:00 to 03:00 local time is skipped), then the schedule gives exactly one occurrence that day, after the skipped hour and no later than 03:30 local time, and the next one is 02:30 the following day.
- Given the same cron in Utc mode, then that day's occurrence is 02:30 UTC, as on any other day (REQ-200's notes: daylight-saving rules apply only to Local jobs).
- Property: for random daily cron expressions that name one time of day, and random zones over many years, a named time that falls in a gap gives exactly one occurrence on that day, at or after the end of the gap.
- Which instant "the next valid instant" is, and whether several named times inside one gap give one run or several, is open question 1.

### AC-4 · REQ-229 · must

> Where a recurring job's time mode is Local, when its next occurrence falls on a local time repeated by a daylight-saving change, the system shall run it once, at the first instance of that time.
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06: the first instance. Interval schedules are INV-208.

- Given Local mode for Europe/Berlin and a daily cron at 02:30, when the next occurrence falls on the autumn change (02:00 to 03:00 local time is repeated), then it occurs once, at 02:30 summer time (00:30 UTC), and the occurrence after it is 02:30 the following day, not 02:30 winter time (01:30 UTC).
- Given an hourly cron (minute 0 of every hour) in Local mode on that day, then 02:00 occurs once, at its first instance, so two hours of elapsed time pass before 03:00; an interval schedule does not do this (INV-208).
- Property: for random cron expressions and zones over many years, every local time a schedule names inside a repeated hour occurs exactly once, at its first instance, and the occurrences are strictly increasing.
- Given Utc mode, then no occurrence is moved or dropped on that day.

### AC-5 · TECH-23 · must

> **Schedule syntax** — The recurring scheduler shall accept 5- and 6-field cron expressions (seconds optional), fixed intervals with Every(TimeSpan), and readable helpers such as Cron.Daily(hour: 3).

- Test: a 5-field cron expression and a 6-field one with a seconds field are both accepted and give the expected occurrences; Every(TimeSpan) gives a fixed interval (INV-208); Cron.Daily(hour: 3) gives 03:00 every day in the job's time mode (REQ-200).
- Given a text that is not a 5- or 6-field cron expression (for example one with four fields), then it is not accepted as a schedule; how the call is rejected is recurring REQ-209 (ST-023) and REQ-210 (ST-022).
- The dialect beyond the field count, the other helpers and the TimeSpans Every accepts are open question 3.

### AC-6 · TECH-24 · must

> **Time zone identifiers** — The recurring scheduler shall identify time zones by IANA time zone id.

- Test: a zone given by IANA id (Europe/Berlin, America/New_York, Etc/UTC) is accepted and evaluated with that zone's rules.
- Given a Windows zone id such as W. Europe Standard Time, then it is not accepted as a zone on any platform the tests run on, even where the platform could convert it; recurring REQ-209 and REQ-210 reject it as not a valid IANA zone.
- Where the zone rules come from is open question 5.

### AC-7 · TECH-25 · should

> **ISchedule extension point** — The core package shall define ISchedule with GetNextOccurrence(after, timeZone), with cron and interval as built-in implementations, so applications can add calendars.

- Test: an application's own ISchedule (a calendar that fires on listed dates) is accepted wherever a schedule is (AddRecurring, TECH-22), and its GetNextOccurrence(after, timeZone) alone decides its occurrences; the trigger that calls it is ST-026.
- Inspection: the built-in cron and interval schedules implement ISchedule, and nothing in the core evaluates a schedule except through GetNextOccurrence.
- What GetNextOccurrence returns when a schedule has no further occurrence is open question 3, and how a stored definition records an application's own schedule is open question 4.

## Open questions

1. **Decide in delivery** · REQ-228: is "the next valid instant" the end of the gap (03:00 for a skipped 02:30 in Europe/Berlin) or the requested time moved by the gap's length (03:30), and do several named times inside one gap give one run or one each? The criteria hold under both readings.
2. **Decide in delivery** · INV-208, TECH-25: what is an interval anchored to when GetNextOccurrence is called with an after that is not an occurrence (after a pause, a misfire or a restart): after plus the interval, or the next point of a grid fixed when the job was defined? It decides when an interval job fires under ST-026's misfire rules.
3. **Decide in delivery** · TECH-23, TECH-25, REQ-200: the formats and types the specs leave open: the cron dialect beyond the field count (where the seconds field goes, special characters and names, whether Sunday is 0 or 7, how day of month and day of week combine), whether Every accepts a zero, negative or very small TimeSpan, the helpers besides Cron.Daily(hour: 3), how a time mode and a zone are given, the types of after, timeZone and the result, and what GetNextOccurrence returns when there is no next occurrence. Expressions are stored (recurring REQ-203), and NFR-20 later freezes them.
4. **Decide in delivery** · TECH-25: how does a stored definition record a schedule that is an application's own ISchedule? Cron expressions and intervals have a text form and a calendar does not, yet runtime-defined jobs (TECH-21) and the definition hash (recurring REQ-203) need one.
5. **Decide in delivery** · TECH-24, REQ-200: where do the zone rules come from? TECH-8 rules out a third-party zone package, so they come from each host's own tz data, and nodes on different tz database versions, or a container without tz data, can compute different instants for one Local job.
