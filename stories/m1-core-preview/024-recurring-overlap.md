# ST-024 — Start recurring occurrences under each overlap policy

**Milestone:** M1 · **Epic:** m1-core-preview · **Priority:** must · **Depends on:** ST-023, ST-018, ST-019

## Context

Gives recurring jobs occurrences and all four overlap policies. An occurrence is created and decided against the earlier occurrences of the same recurring job: it starts when nothing is in the way (REQ-221). Under Skip it is skipped (REQ-222). Under Queue it waits, up to MaxQueuedOccurrences (REQ-223, REQ-233), or is skipped with reason QueueFull beyond that (REQ-235). Waiting occurrences start one at a time in creation order (REQ-224). Allow and CancelPrevious always start it, and CancelPrevious asks the earlier run to stop (REQ-225). Every start cancels an earlier occurrence's Unroutable job with reason Superseded (REQ-240, SCN-215). "In the way" uses REQ-239's meaning of unfinished. Together these keep two occurrence jobs of a Skip or Queue job from processing at once (INV-204), and no overlap decision ever fails a job, throws or waits on a lock (INV-205). In this story occurrences come from trigger now (REQ-226).

Builds on ST-023 (trigger now on IRecurringJobManager, TECH-21) and ST-022 (the stored definition, its payload and its policies, REQ-201). It also builds on ST-018 (cancellation, which CancelPrevious and REQ-240 use, and through it the Failed, DeadLettered and Cancelled states of REQ-239 from ST-011 and ST-018). It builds on ST-019 too (uniqueness, because an occurrence's job is created like any other job). It keeps jobs REQ-134 holding (CancelPrevious and REQ-240 cancel enqueued, failed and unroutable jobs through it). It keeps jobs REQ-150 holding as well (its duplicate rules cover recurring occurrences, per its notes). REQ-240 sits here with REQ-239, so INV-204 is attacked with jobs REQ-125's return of an Unroutable job included. The scheduled trigger, REQ-216, is ST-026, the last recurring story, because REQ-216, INV-212 and REQ-242 depend on each other and on every other recurring write. ST-026 sends scheduled occurrences through these decisions (SCN-201, SCN-210, SCN-213) and keeps INV-204 holding. ST-025 adds the skipping of waiting occurrences when a job stops (REQ-237) and the last outcome (REQ-231).

Lives in the Bellhop core package (TECH-112). Occurrence records (state Waiting, Started or Skipped, with scheduledFor, job and reason, per the recurring entities) go into the in-memory store (TECH-43) behind IJobStore (TECH-31). An occurrence's job is enqueued through the jobs module's enqueue path (ST-007). Cancel reasons are recorded with the job's state transition (jobs INV-116). The criteria build to recurring A-30: a trigger now's decision and a REQ-224 start are each one atomic step. How a store makes them so is open question 11. Queue's maximum is set per job with the overlap policy, as Queue(max) (REQ-233's notes); the option's name is ST-022's open question 1. Open questions 1 and 7 cover the reason recorded under Skip and what trigger now returns.

## Acceptance criteria

### AC-1 · REQ-233 · must

> The system shall allow at most MaxQueuedOccurrences waiting occurrences per recurring job under the Queue overlap policy, 1 by default (MaxQueuedOccurrences).
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06: configurable per job as Queue(max).

- Given overlap Queue with no maximum and an earlier occurrence whose job is processing, when two more occurrences are created, then the first waits and the second is skipped with reason QueueFull (REQ-235).
- Given Queue(3) on one job and plain Queue on another, then the first holds up to three waiting occurrences and skips the fourth with reason QueueFull, while the second still holds one.
- Property: over random sequences of occurrences, job completions and waiting starts, a Queue(max) job never has more than max waiting occurrences. The generator holds each job's policy fixed for the run (recurring A-9) until the owner answers ST-023's open question 4.
- What Queue(max) with a maximum below 1 does is open question 6.

### AC-2 · REQ-239 · must

> The system shall treat an occurrence's job as unfinished for overlap decisions while it is not Succeeded, DeadLettered, Cancelled or Unroutable.
>
> *Notes:* refiner-model 2026-11-06 F-7 (decided 2026-11-06, recommended option): an Unroutable occurrence job no longer blocks its recurring job for good; the next occurrence is decided by its own handler availability and becomes Unroutable too, which the dashboard shows. Before this, the definition (without Unroutable) lived only in REQ-221's notes and the glossary, which are not semantic. Run 2 F-1: because jobs REQ-125 brings an Unroutable job back when a handler appears, the newer occurrence cancels it when it starts (REQ-240), so the two never run side by side.

- Given overlap Skip and an earlier occurrence whose job is Enqueued, Processing, or Failed and waiting for its retry, when an occurrence is created, then it is skipped: that job is unfinished (REQ-221's notes: a run waiting to retry blocks the next one).
- Given the earlier occurrence's job is Succeeded, DeadLettered, Cancelled or Unroutable, then the new occurrence starts.
- Given the earlier job was marked Cancelled while its abandoned handler still runs (jobs REQ-190), then it counts as finished. The handler that keeps running is the window REQ-238 accepts.
- Given the earlier job goes from Unroutable back to Enqueued (jobs REQ-125), then it is unfinished again from then on.
- What an earlier occurrence's job that was deleted while unfinished counts as is open question 5.

### AC-3 · REQ-221 · must

> When an occurrence is created and no earlier occurrence of the same recurring job is waiting or has an unfinished job, the system shall enqueue a job with the recurring job's payload and record the occurrence as started.
>
> *Transition:* Occurrence: (new) → Started · *Actor:* system
>
> *Notes:* Recurring jobs may carry a payload, so one job type can have many recurring instances, for example one per tenant (REC-8). Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06: 'still running' means the occurrence's job is unfinished, as defined in REQ-239. A run waiting to retry therefore blocks the next one. refiner-model 2026-11-06 F-1 (decided 2026-11-06, recommended option): a waiting occurrence is also in the way, so a new occurrence never starts ahead of one that is still waiting. F-7: Unroutable no longer counts as unfinished (REQ-239).

- Given a recurring job with no earlier occurrence that is waiting or has an unfinished job, when an occurrence is created, then a job is enqueued with the recurring job's stored payload (an edited one if REQ-212 changed it) and the occurrence is recorded as Started with that job. This holds whatever the overlap policy.
- Given two recurring jobs of the same job type with different payloads, for example one per tenant, then each one's occurrences are decided only against its own earlier occurrences (REQ-221's notes).
- Given a removed job that was created again under the same name while a job of one of its old occurrences is unfinished, then the new job's occurrences are decided against that old job (recurring A-34).
- Given overlap Skip or Queue and an earlier occurrence that is waiting, then a new occurrence never starts ahead of it, even if no earlier job is unfinished.
- An earlier occurrence's Unroutable job is cancelled with reason Superseded in the same write (REQ-240). Whether the decision and the start are one conditional store write is open question 11. A job type with a unique key is open questions 9 and 10.

### AC-4 · REQ-222 · must

> When an occurrence is created while an earlier occurrence of the same recurring job is waiting or has an unfinished job, if the overlap policy is Skip, then the system shall start nothing and record the occurrence as skipped.
>
> *Transition:* Occurrence: (new) → Skipped · *Actor:* system
>
> *Notes:* refiner-model 2026-11-06 F-1: a waiting earlier occurrence counts as in the way, the same as an unfinished job.

- Given overlap Skip and an earlier occurrence whose job is processing, when an occurrence is created, then it is recorded as Skipped, no job is enqueued, and the earlier job is still the only one processing.
- Given the earlier job is Failed and waiting for its retry, then the new occurrence is skipped the same way.
- Given nothing earlier is in the way, then this rule does not apply (REQ-221).
- The skip is a recorded outcome: the call that created the occurrence does not throw, and no job is failed (INV-205). The reason recorded is open question 1.

### AC-5 · REQ-223 · must

> When an occurrence is created while an earlier occurrence of the same recurring job is waiting or has an unfinished job, if the overlap policy is Queue and fewer occurrences than its maximum are waiting, then the system shall record the occurrence as waiting for the earlier ones to finish.
>
> *Transition:* Occurrence: (new) → Waiting · *Actor:* system
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06: Queue takes a maximum of waiting occurrences, 1 by default (REQ-233); beyond it occurrences are skipped (REQ-235). refiner-model 2026-11-06 F-1: a waiting earlier occurrence counts as in the way, the same as an unfinished job.

- Given overlap Queue(2), an earlier occurrence whose job is processing, and no occurrence waiting, when an occurrence is created, then it is recorded as Waiting and no job is enqueued for it yet. A second one created the same way also waits.
- Given Queue(2), with the earlier job already succeeded but one occurrence still waiting, when an occurrence is created, then it waits behind the waiting one (a waiting occurrence is in the way, F-1).
- Given the maximum is already reached, then REQ-235 applies instead.

### AC-6 · REQ-224 · must

> While an occurrence is waiting, when no earlier occurrence of the same recurring job is waiting or has an unfinished job, the system shall enqueue a job for the waiting occurrence and record it as started.
>
> *Transition:* Occurrence: Waiting → Started · *Actor:* system
>
> *Notes:* refiner-model 2026-11-06 F-2 (decided 2026-11-06, recommended option): waiting occurrences start one at a time in creation order, each only once every earlier occurrence is finished and none is still waiting, so Queue(max) is a FIFO with one runner whatever the maximum. Replaces 'when the earlier occurrence's job succeeds, is dead-lettered or is cancelled', which released every waiting occurrence at once.

- Given Queue(2), occurrence 1's job processing and occurrences 2 and 3 waiting, when job 1 succeeds, then occurrence 2 is recorded as Started with an enqueued job, and occurrence 3 keeps waiting. When job 2 finishes, occurrence 3 starts. So there is one runner at a time, in creation order.
- Given the earlier job is dead-lettered, cancelled or becomes Unroutable instead, then the waiting occurrence starts the same way (REQ-239).
- Given the earlier job fails and waits for its retry, then the waiting occurrence does not start.
- Concurrency: given several nodes see the earlier job finish at once, then the waiting occurrence starts once and gets exactly one job.
- Which node starts a waiting occurrence, and how soon after the earlier job finishes, is open question 8. This start also supersedes an earlier Unroutable job (REQ-240).

### AC-7 · REQ-225 · must

> When an occurrence is created while an earlier occurrence of the same recurring job is waiting or has an unfinished job, if the overlap policy is Allow or CancelPrevious, then the system shall enqueue a job for the new occurrence and record it as started, and under CancelPrevious also request cancellation of the earlier occurrence's job.
>
> *Transition:* Occurrence: (new) → Started · *Actor:* system
>
> *Notes:* Under CancelPrevious the two runs overlap until the earlier handler honours its token. refiner-model 2026-11-06 F-1: a waiting earlier occurrence counts as in the way, the same as an unfinished job.

- Given overlap Allow and an earlier occurrence whose job is processing, when an occurrence is created, then a job is enqueued for it and it is recorded as Started. The earlier job is left alone, and both may process at once.
- Given overlap CancelPrevious and an earlier occurrence whose job is processing, then the new occurrence is Started with an enqueued job, and the earlier job's cancellation is requested: its handler's token is signalled (jobs REQ-135). Both run until that handler honours the token (notes).
- Given CancelPrevious and the earlier job is Enqueued, or Failed and waiting for its retry, then that job is marked Cancelled at once (jobs REQ-134).
- Given CancelPrevious and the earlier job is already finished, then no cancellation is requested (REQ-221 applies).
- Given Allow or CancelPrevious and an earlier occurrence's job is Unroutable, then it is also superseded in the same write (REQ-240, recurring A-18).
- With several earlier unfinished jobs under CancelPrevious, which ones get the cancellation request is open question 2. Their cancel reason is open question 3.

### AC-8 · REQ-226 · must

> When an operator triggers a recurring job now, optionally with an overridden payload, the system shall create an occurrence immediately, using the overridden payload if one was given, decide it by the overlap policy like a scheduled occurrence, and leave nextRunAt unchanged.
>
> *Transition:* Occurrence: (new) → Started | Waiting | Skipped · *Actor:* Operator
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06: the overlap policy applies, nextRunAt does not move, and it is allowed while the job is paused or orphaned because it is an explicit operator action.

- Given an Active job, when an operator triggers it now through IRecurringJobManager, then an occurrence is created at once and decided by the overlap policy (REQ-221 to REQ-223, REQ-235), and nextRunAt is unchanged.
- Given an overridden payload, then the occurrence's job carries that payload and the stored definition's payload is unchanged. Given none, the job carries the stored payload.
- Given the job is Paused or Orphaned, then trigger now still creates the occurrence, and the job stays Paused or Orphaned.
- Given a code-defined job without AllowRuntimeOverride, then trigger now is allowed (REQ-211's notes).
- Given the maintenance pause is on (ST-009), then the occurrence is still created, because INV-210 covers only occurrences created on schedule. Its job is not claimed until maintenance is off (jobs INV-114).
- Given a trigger now and a pause of the same job at the same moment, then the occurrence is created whatever order they land in, because trigger now changes neither the state nor nextRunAt (INV-212's notes).
- What trigger now returns, and what it does for a name that is not stored, is open question 7.

### AC-9 · REQ-235 · must

> When an occurrence is created while an earlier occurrence of the same recurring job is waiting or has an unfinished job, if the overlap policy is Queue and its maximum of waiting occurrences is already reached, then the system shall start nothing and record the occurrence as skipped with reason QueueFull.
>
> *Transition:* Occurrence: (new) → Skipped · *Actor:* system
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06. Same event as REQ-222, REQ-223 and REQ-225 with a complementary guard. refiner-model 2026-11-06 F-1: a waiting earlier occurrence counts as in the way, the same as an unfinished job.

- Given overlap Queue with the default maximum of 1, an earlier job processing and one occurrence waiting, when an occurrence is created, then it is recorded as Skipped with reason QueueFull. No job is enqueued, and the waiting occurrence and the processing job are unchanged.
- Given Queue(1), with the earlier job already succeeded but its waiting successor not started yet, then a new occurrence is still skipped with reason QueueFull, because the waiting one is in the way (SCN-213 in ST-026).
- Given fewer occurrences than the maximum are waiting, then REQ-223 applies instead.
- Like REQ-222, the skip throws nothing and fails no job (INV-205).

### AC-10 · REQ-240 · must

> When an occurrence of a recurring job starts while an earlier occurrence of the same recurring job has an unroutable job, the system shall cancel that unroutable job with reason Superseded in the same write that starts the new occurrence.
>
> *Transition:* jobs.Job: Unroutable → Cancelled · *Actor:* system
>
> *Rationale:* REQ-239 lets the next occurrence start past an Unroutable job, but jobs REQ-125 returns that job to Enqueued as soon as a handler appears, which is exactly when the newer job becomes runnable too (INV-204).
>
> *Notes:* refiner-model 2026-11-06 run 2 F-1 (recommended option; checked by the model over 262,576 states with all four overlap policies). The newer occurrence replaces the stale one, which is what Skip and Queue intend, so under Skip and Queue a recurring job never holds more than one Unroutable job and only one runs when a handler comes back. Under Allow and CancelPrevious, jobs started side by side can all become Unroutable and all run when it comes back, which those policies permit (run 4 F-3). Cost: the stale occurrence's run is lost; the cancel reason says why. jobs REQ-134 already allows cancelling an Unroutable job, so the jobs module needs no change. Run 3 F-1 (recommended option): the sentence applies to every start under every overlap policy, including Allow and CancelPrevious starts under REQ-225, so whether a stale job is superseded never depends on whether some other earlier job happens to be unfinished. Run 3 F-2 (recommended option, accepted): an earlier trigger-now occurrence (REQ-226) is superseded like a scheduled one, even with an overridden payload; the operator sees reason Superseded and triggers it again once the handler is back. Exempting it would either block the schedule behind its Unroutable job again (run 1 F-7) or let it run beside the newer job (run 2 F-1).

- Given sync-crm with overlap Skip and its earlier occurrence's job Unroutable, when a newer occurrence starts (REQ-221), then the Unroutable job is Cancelled with reason Superseded (recorded with its transition, jobs INV-116), in the same write that starts the new occurrence (SCN-215).
- The same holds for every start: a waiting occurrence that starts (REQ-224), and a start under Allow or CancelPrevious (REQ-225), whatever else is in the way (recurring A-18).
- Given the earlier Unroutable job belongs to an occurrence created by trigger now, even with an overridden payload, then it is superseded the same way (notes, run 3 F-2).
- Given the earlier job is not Unroutable (Processing under Allow, for example), then this rule does not cancel it. Given the new occurrence is skipped or waits, then nothing is superseded, because nothing started.
- Concurrency: given a start racing a worker node that registers the handler (jobs REQ-125), then one of two things happens. Either the old job is cancelled as Superseded and only the new one can be claimed, or the old job was back in Enqueued before the start, in which case it is unfinished and the overlap policy decides the new occurrence against it. Under Skip and Queue two occurrence jobs never both process (INV-204, recurring A-19).
- The cancellation raises IJobEvents' cancelled event once, after the write commits (jobs REQ-169).

### AC-11 · INV-204 · must

> Where a recurring job's overlap policy is Skip or Queue, the system shall never have two of its occurrences' jobs in the Processing state at the same time.
>
> *Notes:* refiner-model 2026-11-06 F-3 (decided 2026-11-06, recommended option): restated in terms of job states in the store. An abandoned or lease-lost handler can still run beside the next occurrence (REQ-238).

- Property and concurrency, run under Skip, Queue and Queue(2) with several worker nodes over one in-memory store and a fake clock, as random interleavings of: trigger nows from several threads, claims, successes, failures with retry, dead-lettering, lease expiry and recovery (jobs REQ-132), timeouts that abandon the handler (jobs REQ-138, REQ-186), cancellations, Unroutable marking (jobs REQ-124), a worker node registering the handler so an Unroutable job returns to Enqueued (jobs REQ-125), supersedes (REQ-240) and waiting starts (REQ-224). Observed: at no point are two of the recurring job's occurrence jobs in the Processing state in the store.
- A handler that was abandoned or lost its lease may still be running beside the next occurrence's job. That is the window REQ-238 accepts, and it is not a failure of this test, which judges store states (recurring A-12).
- Allow and CancelPrevious are not covered: they let occurrences run side by side (REQ-225). An operator's requeue of an earlier occurrence's job is open question 4.
- The generator holds each job's policy fixed for the run (recurring A-9). Whether INV-204 holds from an edit that changes the policy is open question 12.

### AC-12 · INV-205 · must

> The system shall never fail a job, throw an exception or wait on a lock timeout because of an overlap decision.

- Property: over random sequences of occurrences under every overlap policy, including full queues, every overlap decision ends with the occurrence Started, Waiting or Skipped. No decision throws to the caller, and no job is marked Failed because of one. The only other jobs a decision changes are an earlier Unroutable job cancelled as Superseded (REQ-240) and, under CancelPrevious, the earlier job whose cancellation it requests (REQ-225).
- Concurrency: given many trigger nows of one job at once from several threads and nodes, then every call completes, none waits on a lock timeout, and each occurrence ends in one of the three states.
- Inspection: the decision path takes no lock that can time out and uses no distributed-lock library (hosting REQ-37). The reviewer records the path in Notes.

### AC-13 · SCN-215 · scenario

> **A newer occurrence supersedes an Unroutable one**
>
> *Given:* sync-crm runs every minute with overlap Skip. Its 10:00 occurrence's job is Unroutable because no running worker has the SyncCrm handler.
>
> 1. REQ-221 — the 10:06 occurrence is created; the Unroutable job does not count as unfinished (REQ-239)
> 2. REQ-240 — the 10:00 job is cancelled with reason Superseded in the write that starts the 10:06 occurrence
>
> *Then:* The 10:00 job is Cancelled with reason Superseded, the 10:06 occurrence is Started with an Enqueued job, and sync-crm has no Unroutable job, so a worker that later registers SyncCrm can run only the 10:06 job.
>
> *Notes:* refiner-model 2026-11-06 run 2 F-1 regression.
