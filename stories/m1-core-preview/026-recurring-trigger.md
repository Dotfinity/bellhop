# ST-026 — Trigger recurring jobs on schedule, once across nodes, with misfire handling

**Milestone:** M1 · **Epic:** m1-core-preview · **Priority:** must · **Depends on:** ST-025, ST-009, ST-010, ST-021

## Context

Makes recurring jobs run on schedule. While a job is Active and maintenance is off, the job's due nextRunAt is advanced, and the occurrences its misfire policy calls for are created, in one compare-and-set (REQ-216). So each due instant gets at most one occurrence however many nodes poll (INV-203). An instant more than MisfireThreshold late is missed (REQ-234) and is handled by RunOnce, Skip or RunAll (REQ-217 to REQ-219, INV-207). The miss is logged and raised as an event (REQ-220), and recorded in the job store (REQ-242). Pausing, orphaning and maintenance stop occurrences on schedule (INV-201, INV-210). INV-212 makes every recurring write a compare-and-set on what it read; this story owns it and attacks every write in the module with it.

Builds on ST-025 (skipping waiting occurrences and the last outcome) and, through it, ST-024 (every overlap policy and the supersede, so each occurrence a trigger creates is decided), ST-009 (the maintenance pause), ST-010 (IJobEvents and jobs REQ-169) and ST-021 (retention cleanup, which REQ-242's record needs). It adds REQ-242's record to writes built earlier: ST-022's REQ-204, REQ-205, REQ-207 and REQ-241, and ST-023's REQ-212, REQ-213 and REQ-214. It runs those writes, REQ-206 and REQ-215 under INV-212. It keeps holding jobs REQ-169, hosting REQ-40, TECH-45 (adding the trigger's maintenance condition), INV-204 (now for scheduled occurrences too) and jobs REQ-150 (the duplicate rules also cover the jobs a scheduled trigger's occurrences create, per its notes). INV-210 is tagged M2 but owned here by the owner's decision of 2026-11-10 (decision 2): REQ-216 and INV-212 read the maintenance pause, which ST-009 builds in M1. REQ-242 keeps its record as long as RetentionDeadLettered (jobs REQ-104), from ST-021. These stories build on it: ST-027 (Bellhop.Testing), ST-028 (tracing and metrics), ST-030 (PostgreSQL, which constrains TECH-45, INV-203 and INV-212), ST-036 (NFR-14), ST-047 (validity windows, which constrains INV-201), ST-052 (integrations REQ-403 and INV-412 read REQ-242's record), ST-054 (conformance) and ST-058 (the Redis trigger script).

Lives in the Bellhop core package (TECH-112). The trigger is a background duty of the worker that AddWorker() registers (TECH-128, hosting REQ-40). The in-memory store's recurring write (TECH-43, TECH-45) gains the trigger's maintenance condition and REQ-242's record. The event is IJobEvents.OnRecurringMissed (TECH-27), and logging goes through ILogger with stable EventIds (NFR-51). The specs give neither the poll interval, nor MisfireThreshold's option, nor the record's stored shape and the store method that reads it (open questions 1, 2 and 6).

- integrations A-24 (affects REQ-403, recurring REQ-242, REQ-234): A pause, orphaning or removal of the active recurring job with an overdue instant is one action, `PauseMissed`: it writes a REQ-242 record and no last outcome, and stops triggers. A resume writes nothing. Instants that fall due while paused are not missed (REQ-234) and are not modeled.
- recurring A-3 (affects REQ-217–REQ-219, REQ-234, INV-207, REQ-242): The newest due instant is late or on time by nondeterministic choice. With two or more instants due, the older ones are always missed, which assumes the schedule interval exceeds MisfireThreshold.

## Acceptance criteria

### AC-1 · REQ-234 · must

> The system shall treat a due instant as missed when the trigger runs more than MisfireThreshold after it, 60 seconds by default (MisfireThreshold), or, for an instant that fell due while the recurring job was active, when no trigger ran for it before nextRunAt was moved past it or the job stopped being active, however recently it fell due.
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06; same default as Quartz. refiner-model run 5 F-2 (recommended option): 'missed' was defined only by when the trigger runs, so an instant that had just passed when a schedule change, pause, orphaning or removal passed over it was neither run nor missed, and left no trace. Now every instant that passed while the job was active either runs on time or is missed and recorded (REQ-242). Instants that fall due while a job is paused or orphaned are not missed; they are dropped on purpose (REQ-215, REQ-206).

- Given MisfireThreshold is not configured, when a trigger runs 60 s after a due instant, then the instant is on time (INV-207). When the trigger runs 61 s after it, the instant is missed.
- Given MisfireThreshold is configured as 10 s, then 10 s late is on time and 11 s late is missed. The option's name and scope are open question 2.
- Given an Active job whose 02:00 instant fell due 1 s ago and has not been triggered, then that instant is missed, however recently it fell due, when any of these lands first: a pause (REQ-214), a schedule change (REQ-227), an orphaning (REQ-205) or a removal (REQ-207, REQ-213). REQ-242 records it.
- Given an instant fell due while the job was Paused or Orphaned, then it is not missed. After a resume (REQ-215) or a restore (REQ-206) it has no occurrence, no REQ-242 record and no OnRecurringMissed event.
- Given an instant fell due while the job was not stored, then it is not missed (REQ-203's notes).
- Given an instant fell due while maintenance was on, then it is judged by the first clause when the trigger runs after maintenance ends (INV-210).

### AC-2 · REQ-216 · must

> While a recurring job is active and maintenance is off, when its nextRunAt has passed according to the database clock, the system shall in one compare-and-set as INV-212 describes, advance nextRunAt to the next occurrence after now and create the occurrence or occurrences its misfire policy calls for.
>
> *Transition:* RecurringJob: Active → Active · *Actor:* system
>
> *Notes:* refiner-model 2026-11-08 recurring run 6 F-1: the compare-and-set also covers the job's state, because a pause does not move nextRunAt. On nextRunAt only, a trigger that read the job as active could still land after a pause and create an occurrence for a paused job (INV-201). Run 7 F-1: it also requires maintenance to be off at the write. Maintenance is a separate flag, so a trigger that read the job just before an operator turned maintenance on still created its occurrence and advanced nextRunAt while maintenance was on (INV-210); now it loses, rereads, finds maintenance on and does nothing.

- Given nightly-cleanup, daily at 03:00, Active with nextRunAt 03:00 and maintenance off, when a worker node's scheduler polls at 03:00:01 by the database clock, then one write moves nextRunAt to the next occurrence after now (03:00 tomorrow) and creates the 03:00 occurrence, which the overlap policy decides (REQ-221 to REQ-225).
- Given nextRunAt has not passed by the database clock, then nothing is written, whatever the node's own clock says (jobs INV-105).
- Given the job is Paused or Orphaned, then nothing is written (INV-201). Given maintenance is on, then nothing is written either (INV-210).
- Given another node's trigger landed first, then this node's compare-and-set changes nothing. Its reread finds nextRunAt in the future and it creates nothing (SCN-200).
- Given the store write fails, for example with the store unreachable, then neither nextRunAt nor any occurrence has changed, and the failure is logged with a stable EventId (NFR-51). The first poll after the store is back triggers the instant, judged then by REQ-234.
- Given a node stops right after its write landed, then the write's nextRunAt and occurrences are both stored. Given it stops before the write, neither is.
- Given a node without the worker, then it never triggers (hosting REQ-40).

### AC-3 · INV-201 · must

> While a recurring job is paused or orphaned, the system shall never create an occurrence for it on schedule.
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06: 'trigger now' (REQ-226) is allowed while paused or orphaned; this rule covers only occurrences created on schedule.

- Concurrency (SCN-217): given node A's trigger has read nightly-export as Active and due, when an operator's pause lands before A's write, then A's compare-and-set changes nothing. Its reread finds the job Paused, and no occurrence is created. The same holds when an orphaning lands first (REQ-205).
- Property: over random interleavings of triggers (each with its read and write apart), pauses, resumes, orphanings, restores, trigger nows and time passing on several nodes, no occurrence is ever created by a scheduled trigger while the stored job is Paused or Orphaned.
- Observed: the occurrences created on schedule for the job while it was Paused or Orphaned, which must be none (SCN-202). Trigger nows in that time are allowed (REQ-226) and are not counted.
- ST-047's validity window keeps INV-201 holding.

### AC-4 · INV-203 · must

> The system shall create at most one occurrence for each due instant of a recurring job, however many nodes run the scheduler.

- Concurrency: several worker nodes poll one store at the same instant, repeated over many due instants and every misfire policy, with threads interleaved at random. Observed: every due instant of the job is covered by at most one occurrence (SCN-200).
- Concurrency: given a trigger racing a resume (REQ-215), a schedule change (REQ-227) or another trigger, each of which moves nextRunAt, then no instant gets a second occurrence. That would fail without nextRunAt in the compare-and-set (recurring A-2).
- Given a node stops after its trigger landed, then no other node creates a second occurrence for that instant.
- Trigger now's occurrences are not due instants and are not counted (REQ-226). ST-030 runs the same tests on PostgreSQL and keeps INV-203 holding.

### AC-5 · INV-207 · must

> When a recurring job is triggered no more than MisfireThreshold after its due instant, the system shall treat the instant as on time and create its occurrence whatever the misfire policy.
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06, so Skip never drops a run that is only a poll interval late.

- Given three jobs due at 03:00:00 with misfire RunOnce, Skip and RunAll(2), when each is first triggered at 03:00:02, then each creates its 03:00 occurrence. Nothing is logged as missed, no OnRecurringMissed event is raised, and no REQ-242 record is written (SCN-211).
- Given a trigger exactly MisfireThreshold (60 s by default) after the instant, then the same holds. Given 61 s, the instant is missed (REQ-234).
- Property: for random lateness up to MisfireThreshold and every misfire policy, an occurrence is created for the instant. The overlap policy then decides it, and it may be skipped.

### AC-6 · INV-210 · must

> While the global maintenance pause is on, the system shall never create an occurrence on schedule and never advance nextRunAt by a scheduled trigger, so that instants missed meanwhile go through the misfire policy when maintenance ends.
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06 (scope of maintenance, jobs INV-114). Unlike a paused job (REQ-215), missed instants are not dropped. refiner-model 2026-11-06 F-4 (decided 2026-11-06, recommended option): narrowed to the scheduler. Operator and reconciliation actions during maintenance (REQ-206, REQ-215, REQ-227) keep their own 'from now' rules.

- Given an hourly RunOnce job Active with nextRunAt 03:00, an operator turns maintenance on at 02:59 (jobs REQ-162) and off at 05:10 (jobs REQ-163). While it is on, no occurrence is created on schedule and nextRunAt stays 03:00. At the first poll after it ends, 03:00, 04:00 and 05:00 go through RunOnce: one occurrence, one REQ-242 record, and the log and event of REQ-220. Unlike a pause, nothing is dropped.
- Given maintenance ends no more than MisfireThreshold after the newest due instant, then that instant is on time (INV-207).
- Concurrency: given a trigger read the job just before maintenance was turned on and writes after, then its compare-and-set, which also requires maintenance off (TECH-45), changes nothing. Its reread finds maintenance on and it does nothing (REQ-216's notes).
- Given maintenance is on, then a pause, a resume with nextRunAt from now (REQ-215), a schedule change (REQ-227), a restore (REQ-206), REQ-241's update and REQ-203's create all still land (recurring A-20). A trigger now still creates its occurrence (REQ-226).
- Property: over random interleavings with maintenance switched on and off, no trigger write lands while maintenance is on.

### AC-7 · INV-212 · must

> The system shall make every store write that changes a recurring job's state, nextRunAt or definition, or removes the job (a trigger, a schedule change, a definition update by REQ-204, REQ-212 or REQ-241, a pause, a resume, an orphaning, a restore or a removal), a compare-and-set on the state, nextRunAt and definition it read, and for a trigger also on maintenance being off, which judges due and missed instants by the database clock at that write and changes nothing if any of them changed since the read, after which the writer reads the job again and decides anew.
>
> *Rationale:* Every one of these writes decides what it does from what it read: whether an instant runs, whether it was missed, what REQ-242 records, and whether the schedule changed. A write that lands after another one changed any of that acts on a job that no longer exists in that form. The rule is that a write is conditional on everything it decided from, except the heartbeats (notes).
>
> *Notes:* refiner-model 2026-11-08 run 6 F-1 (recommended option, applied to every write instead of the pause alone). The model split the pause into its read and its write: unconditional, a trigger that landed in between ran the 02:00 instant on time and the pause still recorded it as dropped, and integrations REQ-403 alerted a miss that did not happen; a schedule change in between got the instant recorded twice. Recurring writes all follow this one rule rather than each getting a phrase of its own, because the same race also exists in the other direction. A trigger that read the job as active could land after a pause, which does not move nextRunAt, and create an occurrence for a paused job (INV-201); an orphan sweep or a schedule change that read an active job could land after a pause and record its missed instants a second time. The other direction, the orphan, removal, schedule-change and resume writes, and the reread after a lost compare-and-set have not been model-checked yet (SCN-216, SCN-217). A trigger that loses does nothing on its reread, because another node advanced nextRunAt (SCN-200). REQ-226's trigger-now changes neither value, so it is not covered. How a store judges at the write is TECH-45. Run 7 F-1 and F-3 (recommended options, combined): a trigger also decided from maintenance (INV-210), and a definition update decided from the stored schedule whether nextRunAt moves, so both are now in the condition; without them a trigger read just before maintenance turned on still fired, and a REQ-241 update that read an unchanged schedule landed after an operator's schedule edit and left nextRunAt from the edit beside the code schedule, skipping some 20 hours with no record. In a store, the state, nextRunAt and definition are one row version. Run 7 F-2 (option 2): the heartbeats are not part of the condition. The orphan sweep, a restore and a REQ-241 update decide from the declarations they read, and one that changed before the write is corrected at the scheduler's next pass: a job removed while a node that just started declares it is created again (REQ-203), a job orphaned that way is restored (REQ-206), and a definition taken from a node that has just stopped is updated again (REQ-241). The same holds for REQ-203's scheduler create, which can land after the node it took the definition from has stopped; the next pass orphans, removes or updates it as the declarations then say. Comparing heartbeats at the write was not chosen: it needs a cross-table condition, and a node's declarations are published some time after it starts anyway, so it would also need a hosting ordering rule to be complete.

- Attack: for every write INV-212 names, separate the writer's read from its write, land each of the other writes between them (the same write from another node or operator included), and run both orders. The writes are: a trigger (REQ-216), a schedule change or definition update (REQ-204, REQ-212, REQ-227, REQ-241), a pause (REQ-214), a resume (REQ-215), an orphaning (REQ-205), a restore (REQ-206) and a removal (REQ-207, REQ-213). Also turn maintenance on in between for a trigger.
- Observed after each interleaving: a write whose read state, nextRunAt or definition changed before it landed, or (for a trigger) one that finds maintenance on, changed nothing. The writer read the job again and decided anew. The end state is one the same writes produce when applied one at a time in some order. INV-201, INV-203 and INV-210 hold, and REQ-242 recorded no instant twice.
- Regression cases from the notes:
  - SCN-216 and SCN-217.
  - A schedule change that read an overdue 02:00 instant, with a trigger landing in between: 02:00 is recorded once, not twice.
  - An orphaning or schedule change that read an Active job, landing after a pause: the pause's record is not repeated.
  - A REQ-241 update that read an unchanged schedule, landing after an operator's schedule edit: it loses, and afterwards nextRunAt comes from the schedule then stored.
  - A removal and a restore of the same orphan, in both orders.
- Given a pause that read the job before 02:00 fell due and writes after it, with no trigger in between, then 02:00 is still judged at the write and recorded as dropped by the pause. TECH-45's notes give one way to do it.
- Given a sweep, restore, REQ-241 update or REQ-203 create acted on heartbeats that changed before its write, then the scheduler's next pass corrects it, as the notes list. Afterwards the stored job agrees with the running declarations (recurring A-33).
- Trigger now (REQ-226) is not one of these writes, and a trigger now racing any of them still creates its occurrence. INV-212 sets no bound on rereads (findings, Accepted).

### AC-8 · REQ-217 · must

> When a recurring job is triggered after one or more due instants were missed, if the misfire policy is RunOnce, then the system shall create a single occurrence for all missed instants and, if the most recent due instant is on time, for that instant too.
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06: an instant counts as missed only when the trigger runs more than MisfireThreshold (60 s, REQ-234) after it; a slightly late trigger is on time (INV-207). refiner-model 2026-11-06 F-9 (decided 2026-11-06, recommended option): the newest due instant is judged on its own and the catch-up is merged into it, so one trigger never creates a catch-up plus an on-time run that the overlap policy would then skip.

- Given an hourly RunOnce job with every node down from 01:30 to 05:10, when the first poll runs at 05:10, then exactly one occurrence is created for 02:00, 03:00, 04:00 and 05:00, and nextRunAt is 06:00 (SCN-206).
- Given the nodes came back at 05:00:30 instead, so 05:00 is on time, then still exactly one occurrence is created. It covers the missed 02:00 to 04:00 and the on-time 05:00, with no separate on-time run.
- Given only one instant was missed, then one occurrence is created for it.
- Property: over random outages and lateness, a RunOnce trigger creates exactly one occurrence. The overlap policy decides it, and if it is skipped REQ-242's record says so.

### AC-9 · REQ-218 · must

> When a recurring job is triggered after one or more due instants were missed, if the misfire policy is Skip, then the system shall create no occurrence for the missed instants, create the occurrence of the most recent due instant only if it is on time (INV-207), and advance nextRunAt to the next future occurrence.
>
> *Notes:* refiner-model 2026-11-06 F-9: the old 'only advance nextRunAt' contradicted INV-207 when the newest instant was on time.

- Given an hourly job with misfire Skip and every node down from 01:30 to 05:10, when the first poll runs at 05:10, then no occurrence is created and nextRunAt is 06:00.
- Given the nodes came back at 05:00:30 instead, then only the 05:00 occurrence is created (on time, INV-207). None is created for 02:00, 03:00 or 04:00, and nextRunAt is 06:00.
- Property: under misfire Skip, no occurrence is ever created for an instant the trigger reached more than MisfireThreshold late, and after every trigger nextRunAt is in the future.

### AC-10 · REQ-219 · must

> When a recurring job is triggered after one or more due instants were missed, if the misfire policy is RunAll with a maximum, then the system shall create one occurrence per missed instant and one for the most recent due instant if it is on time, keeping the most recent of them up to that maximum, and log the older instants it dropped.
>
> *Notes:* Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06: the newest instants are kept. refiner-model 2026-11-06 F-9: the on-time instant counts toward the maximum.

- Given an hourly job with misfire RunAll(2) and every node down from 01:30 to 05:10, so four instants are missed, then occurrences are created for 04:00 and 05:00. The dropped 02:00 and 03:00 are logged.
- Given RunAll(3) and the nodes back at 05:00:30, so 05:00 is on time, then occurrences are created for 03:00, 04:00 and 05:00, because the on-time instant counts toward the maximum. The dropped 02:00 is logged.
- Given RunAll(5) and four missed instants, then four occurrences are created and nothing is dropped or logged as dropped.
- Property: a RunAll(max) trigger never creates more than max occurrences, and the ones it creates are always the most recent instants.
- Each occurrence is decided by the overlap policy. The order they are created and decided in is open question 4, and RunAll's default and smallest maximum are open question 3.

### AC-11 · REQ-220 · must

> When a recurring job is triggered after one or more due instants were missed, the system shall log how the missed instants were handled and why, and raise the recurring-missed event.
>
> *Notes:* The event is consumed by IJobEvents (jobs REQ-169). Notifications (integrations REQ-403) read the stored record of REQ-242 instead, so a miss is still alerted after a lease handover or while no alerts node runs (integrations INV-412).

- Given SCN-206's trigger, then it is logged with a stable EventId (NFR-51) that the four missed instants were caught up by one RunOnce occurrence, and why: the trigger ran more than MisfireThreshold after them.
- Also, every registered IJobEvents handler's OnRecurringMissed (TECH-27) is called once, on the node whose write landed, after that write committed (jobs REQ-169).
- Given misfire Skip, then the log says the missed instants were dropped by the misfire policy. Given RunAll, it says which instants got occurrences and which were dropped (REQ-219).
- Given an on-time trigger (SCN-211), then nothing is logged as missed and no event is raised.
- Given a node's compare-and-set loses, then that node neither logs nor raises anything for the instants. Only the node whose write landed does.
- Given an OnRecurringMissed handler throws, then the handler's exception is logged, and the trigger's nextRunAt, occurrences and REQ-242 record stay as its write left them (jobs INV-136).
- Whether a pause, orphaning, removal or schedule change that passes over a missed instant also logs and raises the event is open question 5.

### AC-12 · REQ-242 · must

> When an active recurring job's nextRunAt is moved past one or more missed due instants, by a trigger (REQ-216) or by a schedule change (REQ-227), or an active recurring job is paused (REQ-214), orphaned (REQ-205) or removed (REQ-207, REQ-213) while it has missed due instants, the system shall record in the job store which due instants were missed (the first, the last and how many) and how they were handled (the occurrences the misfire policy created for them and whether each started, is waiting or was skipped by the overlap policy, or that the misfire policy, the schedule change, the pause, the orphaning or the removal dropped them), in the same store write that moves nextRunAt or pauses, orphans or removes the job, which is a compare-and-set that judges the missed instants by the database clock at that write (INV-212), and keep that record as long as dead-lettered jobs are kept (jobs RetentionDeadLettered).
>
> *Rationale:* integrations INV-412 makes the notification lease holder find missed occurrences in the job store. The last outcome (REQ-231) is overwritten by the next one, and RunOnce leaves no trace of a miss at all.
>
> *Notes:* refiner-model 2026-11-06 integrations run 3 F-17 (recommended option). One record per trigger, not per instant, so a long outage on a frequent schedule writes one record. REQ-220's log line and in-memory event are unchanged. Run 4 F-1 (recommended option): a schedule change applied at a node start (REQ-204), by the scheduler (REQ-241) or by an operator (REQ-212) moves nextRunAt past overdue instants without a trigger (REQ-227); those instants are recorded as dropped by the schedule change, so integrations REQ-403 alerts them. Before, whether a missed run left any trace depended on whether the deploy also changed the schedule. Instants that fall due while a job is paused or orphaned are dropped on purpose and not recorded (REQ-215, REQ-206). Run 4 F-2 (recommended option): the record says what became of each catch-up occurrence, because the overlap policy can skip it in the same write, and a record saying only "caught up by RunOnce" then reported a run that never happened. Run 5 F-1 (recommended option): instants already missed when an active job is paused, orphaned or removed are recorded in that write. A pause, orphaning or removal does not move nextRunAt, and the later resume or restore happens while the job is not active, so before this those misses left no trace, depending on whether the trigger or the operator came first. Run 5 F-2: 'missed' covers an instant that passed moments before any of these writes and was not triggered yet (REQ-234), so every instant that passed while the job was active either runs or is recorded. Run 5 F-3 (recommended option): the record describes each occurrence as it was in the write that made it; a waiting occurrence may later start (REQ-224) or be skipped (REQ-237), which the record does not follow. The occurrence's own state says what became of it. Run 6 F-1 (recommended option, widened to every write): run 5 made the pause, orphan and removal writes decide the record from what they read, but nothing made them conditional on it, so a trigger that landed in between ran the instant on time and the pause still recorded it as dropped, and integrations REQ-403 alerted a miss that did not happen. Every write that changes the job's state or nextRunAt is now one compare-and-set on both (INV-212).

- Trigger: given SCN-206's trigger, then the same write stores one record. It holds first 02:00, last 05:00 and four instants, handled by one RunOnce occurrence that started. That is one record per trigger, not one per instant.
- Given misfire Skip with the nodes back at 05:00:30, then the record covers 02:00 to 04:00 only and says the misfire policy dropped them. The on-time 05:00 is not missed (recurring A-23) and is not in it.
- Given RunAll(2) with overlap Skip and four instants missed, then the record lists the occurrences created for 04:00 and 05:00 and how each was decided: one started and the other was skipped by the overlap policy (which one is open question 4). It also says the misfire policy dropped 02:00 and 03:00.
- Given a RunOnce catch-up that the overlap policy skips, then the record says skipped, not caught up.
- Given an occurrence the record lists as waiting later starts (REQ-224) or is skipped (REQ-237), then the record is unchanged. It is a snapshot (notes, run 5 F-3).
- Schedule change: given an Active job whose 02:00 instant is overdue, when its schedule is changed by an operator (REQ-212), at a node start (REQ-204) or by REQ-241, then that write stores a record saying the schedule change dropped 02:00.
- Pause, orphaning, removal: given an Active job whose 02:00 instant is overdue, when it is paused (REQ-214), orphaned (REQ-205) or removed (REQ-207, REQ-213), then that write stores a record saying the pause, the orphaning or the removal dropped it.
- No record is written by an on-time trigger, by any of those writes on an Active job with no overdue instant, by any write on a Paused or Orphaned job, or by a resume or a restore (integrations A-24). A write whose compare-and-set loses writes no record (SCN-216).
- Property: over random sequences of every recurring operation with time advancing, each missed instant is in at most one record. Every instant that fell due while the job was Active either has an occurrence or is in a record.
- Retention: given a record written at day 0, then with a fake clock it is still in the store just before RetentionDeadLettered has passed (30 days by default, jobs REQ-104), and also after the job itself was removed (recurring A-21). Once RetentionDeadLettered has passed since it was written, retention cleanup (ST-021) removes it, as it removes dead-lettered jobs.
